Infinity has always been fascinating to me. Transfinite cardinals and ordinals, aleph-null and omega–though detached from reality, these concepts reflect what I believe is the foundation of innovation. Most major advancements come from imagining what might happen in an abstract world beyond our own. I’ve used quaternions extensively when modding Minecraft and rendering 3D models, yet their usefulness comes from being a 4D extension of complex numbers, far beyond our 3D reality. By using abstract concepts, mathematics can reduce any problem to its simplest components, and computer science can implement an algorithm powered by that maths.

When participating in the 2017 FIRST Lego League Challenge, I saw how maths and code could work together to control a robot to perform specific hydrodynamics-related tasks. Since then, I have developed many physics simulations which also demonstrate the synergy of maths and computing–using code and applied mathematics to model the real world. For my Computing A-Level NEA project, I learned how graph theory and matrix algebra can be applied to implement mesh-loop analysis and create an electrical circuit simulator. This is why I wish to study a Maths and Computer Science joint degree–to become an expert problem solver both theoretically and practically.
